FinKit privacy

Last updated: 1 November 2026

FinKit is a personal tool that one person uses to look at their own bank accounts. It is not offered to the public and has no other users.

What FinKit reads

  • Account names (with only the last digits), balances and transactions of the owner's own accounts.
  • This happens only after the owner approves access at their bank, through the open-banking service Enable Banking.
  • FinKit never sees or stores the owner's bank login or password.

Where the data goes

  • Transactions are shown in the owner's browser. The FinKit server does not keep them.
  • The server keeps the bank connection itself, encrypted, so it can fetch new transactions later.
  • The owner's browser may keep a local copy of the transactions so the page can be reopened. It can be switched off and cleared in the app.
  • Nothing is sold, shared with other people, or used for advertising or profiling.

Control and deletion

  • A bank connection can be removed in FinKit or in the bank's own app at any time. Removing it in FinKit deletes the stored connection.
  • Access to each bank expires on its own, after at most 180 days, unless approved again.
